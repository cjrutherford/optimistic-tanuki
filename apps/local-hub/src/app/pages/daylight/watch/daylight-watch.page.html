Daylight builds {{ name() }}'s briefing from the town's own agendas, minutes and notices. Those say what was scheduled and what was filed. They do not say what happened in the room. That part only exists if someone who was there writes it down.

What we are asking

Go to a meeting you were going to hear about anyway, and write a few sentences about what happened, in your own words. Ten minutes. No schedule to keep, nothing to sign up for, and you can withdraw anything you send at any time. You are not being asked to investigate anyone or to become a reporter.

Why one person is not enough

One account of a meeting is one person's account, and we publish it as exactly that — your words, under your handle, never rewritten into ours. It becomes part of the town's record only when people who do not know each other say the same thing and a later document bears it out.

One person saying it is a rumour. Several people who don't know each other saying it, and the minutes agreeing a fortnight later, is a record.

So the honest version of the ask: {{ name() }} needs several people doing this, and we are asking you to be one.

What you should know before you start

  • Your words are published as yours, under whatever handle you choose, and are never rewritten.
  • Everything is reviewed automatically before it appears, and every decision comes with its reason. A report that alleges wrongdoing, names a private individual, or mentions a child waits for evidence rather than being published on one account.
  • There is no human appeal. When the automated review gets it wrong, the only recourse is more evidence.
  • Reposting a news article is refused automatically. Quoting a paragraph with credit is fine.
  • If you have an interest in the matter — you applied for the permit, you work for the department — say so. It does not disqualify your report. Hiding it would.
  • A briefing quotes almost nothing that is sent. Only an officially confirmed account, or a report that independent people corroborated and a later record bore out, is ever quoted. Everything else lives on the town's community page, labeled as what it is.
