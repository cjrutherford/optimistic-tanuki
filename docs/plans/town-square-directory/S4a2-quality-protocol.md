## Decisions received and remaining protocol

The user selected Douglas, Tifton, Valdosta and Waycross and subsequently chose **every eligible record**, reviewed independently by both reviewers, without a per-town limit. This supersedes the earlier 400-record sample plan and applies regardless of frame size. The workload is twice the number of eligible records, plus evidence comparison and the complete proposed-merge review; the frame may contain tens of thousands of records. This frame decision does not itself approve acquisition, the complete protocol or spending.

The user chose the disagreement process: both reviewers independently record their assessments first, then compare evidence and document agreement or unresolved disagreement. An unresolved disagreement makes the affected part of the pilot inconclusive; a third adjudicator is not required. Any outside evidence or checking that could incur a charge must be included in the shared $25 monthly budget and separately authorized where required.

The final protocol must specify the frozen eligible frame, handling of unresolved eligibility, evidence sources and review procedures. Report counts and outcomes for each town and contributor as well as overall. Each town must independently meet the applicable limits to pass; a combined pass cannot override a town failure. A town with no eligible records is inconclusive for listing quality; assess other towns independently. Define town assignment for merge decisions in the frozen rubric. Every eligible record has inclusion probability one; no random allocation or sample seed is required for this record census. Both reviewers also independently examine every proposed merge; record the complete merge frame and its denominator. If reliable evidence cannot be found using freely accessible sources, the affected assessment is inconclusive.

## Proposed acceptance design

After S4a.1 approval identifies a release and permits the specific private operations, define the eligible frame from its exact immutable artifact fingerprints and the selected town boundaries. Record exclusions, frame size, contributor chains and release/schema versions without recording personal details. Assign every eligible record to one town and one primary contributor stratum using a deterministic, documented rule; keep the other attributes as overlapping review tags. Include geographic diversity and the full range of contributor mixtures, sparse entries, potential duplicates, apparent closures and potentially sensitive categories. Unknown provenance, ambiguous location and missing required fields remain in the frame as blocked outcomes; do not silently remove them from quality denominators.

Before review, a human reviewer should approve the final frame, the definitions and thresholds below, and the evidence sources for independent verification. Freeze the frame and document its digest and exclusions. Independently assess every eligible record twice. Tag sensitive, sparse, duplicate-candidate and apparent-closure records for reporting and follow-up; do not count a record twice in the overall denominator. If either reviewer cannot complete the eligible frame, or eligibility or review outcomes remain unresolved, report the affected assessment as inconclusive.

Two independent reviewers classify records against a prewritten rubric, compare evidence after their independent assessments, and mark unresolved disagreements inconclusive. Do not infer closure or other facts from provider confidence or operating-status signals unless their lineage and intended use are separately permitted. Record aggregate results and disagreements, not personal/residential information. Treat adult-related listings as blocked absent explicit opt-in and require a separate review of sensitive locations; source permission alone is insufficient.

## Proposed measures and decision rules

The user approved the 1% duplicate, 5% junk, 10% missing-required-fields and 5% wrong-town and 2% incorrect-merge maxima; the complete acceptance standard is not yet approved. They must be fixed before the acceptance review begins. Define junk as a record that cannot reasonably represent a distinct business in the selected town, duplicate as a record representing the same business location as another record in the frame under the approved matching rubric, and missing required fields as missing or unpublishable name, business location, category, or required attribution. For record measures, report the observed counts and rates for the entire frozen eligible frame and separately by town and contributor. Sampling confidence intervals do not apply to a complete census of that frozen frame; incomplete reviews are inconclusive and the findings do not automatically generalize to other releases or future records. The complete proposed-merge frame is likewise a census; its findings do not automatically generalize to future merge decisions.

Classify geographic mismatches against the approved town-assignment rubric, and false merges against independently reviewed pairs where the pilot has proposed a merge. Report suspected closures and independently verified closure errors as separate counts and rates with denominators and uncertainty; if the source or review process cannot establish closure reliably, do not present a closure status or claim closure accuracy. Freeze and account for every proposed merge and have both reviewers independently assess each one; missing evidence or unresolved disagreement makes the affected assessment inconclusive. If there are no proposed merges, report that check as not applicable without claiming the merge process has been validated.

Separately require zero observed unauthorized fields or unauthorized surface disclosures, and zero unreviewed adult or sensitive-location disclosures. A complete review supports conclusions only for the frozen frame and assessed surfaces; it does not prove zero risk for future records or untested surfaces. Report blocked, quarantined, missing, duplicate and unresolved records in the denominator and by town/contributor stratum. If a measured rate exceeds its approved threshold, coverage is inadequate, reviewers disagree without resolution, or any disclosure test fails, the result is **fail or inconclusive**, not acceptance. ## Pilot and release boundaries

Synthetic fixtures can test evaluation, projection, withdrawal and denial semantics, but cannot establish source quality, live API behavior or licensing. Actual acceptance requires the approved S4a.1 source-use matrix and release manifest; a private rights-enforcing data path; reviewer-approved, frozen sampling protocol; real full-frame review and complete proposed-merge review; and evidence for attribution and withdrawal across all eventual serving surfaces. Public release remains a later S4a.3 decision and exports require their own S8 grant.
