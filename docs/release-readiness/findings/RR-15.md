# RR-15 — Federation and self-hosting feasibility audit

**Status:** GAP FOUND — the committed OT/TS baseline has no federation protocol or portable identity implementation, and the review corpus does not support the identity, privacy, governance, threat-model, or operational decisions needed to start an MVP without reopening foundational questions. This is an audit finding, not an ADR or architecture approval.

**Reviewed baseline:** OT commit `50b82b40c7b73855107fe52f7f1794e656626ac7` (2026-10-13). The working tree also contained unrelated edits to `.gitignore`, three Compose files, and `tsconfig.base.json`, plus untracked `apps/directory-*` and `libs/directory-*` work. These were excluded from inspection. The absence statements below refer to the committed tracked `apps/` and `libs/` baseline only; the untracked directory work was not evaluated. No private operational configuration was opened. No services, tests, builds, peer APIs, or migration commands were run.

## Scope and evidence

Reviewed the RR-15 slice and master criteria; HAI public-good philosophy; RR-11 account lifecycle and DF-007; RR-14 findings; authentication/profile/social/forum/chat/assets models and access code; and the tracked architecture and Compose guides. A committed-source search for ActivityPub, ActivityStreams, WebFinger, AT Protocol, DID, actor inbox/outbox, and federation terms found no protocol implementation in tracked `apps/` or `libs/`. Source and unit-test presence are not treated as runtime acceptance.

The relevant current model is platform-local: authentication users and profiles use UUIDs; profiles can be scoped by `appScope`; social posts, comments, communities, memberships, follows, forum threads/posts, conversations, messages, and assets use local UUIDs and app/profile/user IDs. `Community` has local owner IDs, `appScope`, locality fields, `isPrivate` and join policy. `Post` has public/followers/community visibility and `publicAudienceExplicit`; existing local visibility and asset restrictions are relevant groundwork, but do not authorize cross-instance delivery. Chat conversations are local and messages store ordinary content; no remote actor/address or federation encryption contract was found. Asset records use local storage strategies and local audience restrictions, not remote fetch/cache or tombstone behavior. These observations do not replace previously accepted RR-04 privacy evidence.

Deployment docs describe the existing microservice platform and a full local Compose stack using PostgreSQL, Redis, SeaweedFS, gateway, clients and services. They do not establish a supported minimal third-party self-host profile, version/upgrade compatibility, portability of configuration/data, restore process, support/abuse bootstrap, or independent-server interoperability. No runtime availability or operability claim was tested.

## Feasibility and next decision

The committed platform is a plausible host for an eventual public-content federation adapter, but the current local IDs, local audience/authorization model, account lifecycle gaps, and broad service topology make a drop-in protocol layer unproven. Protocol selection is not made here. ActivityPub looks like the nearer candidate for public social interaction; AT Protocol offers useful portable identity/repository patterns but its public repository model is a poor fit for private content; a custom protocol is not justified by evidence so far. These are research findings, not a product decision.

The next bounded decision work should settle, in order: (1) audience and consent taxonomy, including what can ever leave an instance and what UI disclosure is required; (2) global actor/object ID authority, account migration/domain loss, and how app-scoped profiles relate to actors; (3) explicit public-only first release scope, with DMs and private/follower-scoped content excluded; (4) community/forum ownership and remote moderation authority; (5) TS locality publication/provenance boundaries; and (6) instance threat model and acceptable operator burden. Then produce a separately reviewed ADR and a small F0 export/portability or F1 public-read proposal. That proposal must include schema/migration impact, deletion behavior, security tests, two-instance interoperability, and an operationally supported topology. It does not authorize implementation now. Private/follower federation and messaging remain later, separate decisions.

RR-14's local actor/domain evidence does not supply global identity, and its rights/placement limits remain in force. There is no HAI-wide score, no portable reputation requirement, and no permission to copy prototype source. Conceptual study and independent implementation remain distinct from source transfer.
