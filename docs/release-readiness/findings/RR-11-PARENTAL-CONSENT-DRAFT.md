**Status:** Internal policy/process draft; the selected age gate and parent-attestation flow are implemented in the current working tree with adult/under-13/teen browser flows verified locally; the current-source active-socket withdrawal check passed on the isolated stack. This is not approved public policy, legal advice, a deployed release, or RR-11 acceptance.

**Date:** 2026-10-14

**Scope:** Owner-selected draft age direction and proposed consent states/acceptance gates for Optimistic Tanuki and Towne Square only. Do not apply this direction to other HAI products without an explicit owner decision.

> The owner selected the same age direction for OT and TS: adults 18+; users ages 13–17 only with explicit parent/guardian permission; under 13 unsupported. The owner selected private, self-reported date of birth for new registrations only; existing accounts remain on the legacy path. One simple parent/guardian attestation applies across both apps without independent identity or guardianship verification. It records a claim of authority and permission, not proof. The local implementation keeps a pending applicant inactive, sends a private one-use guardian link, and creates no account/profile/session until attestation and applicant continuation. The permission record retains DOB, guardian contact, attestation text/version, timestamps, status, and an account reference after attachment while permission is needed; it is deleted within 90 days after withdrawal, closure, or age 18. Unapproved pending requests expire within 90 days of creation. This is not blanket publication approval. This draft does not publicize the guardian workflow or inbox.

## Decision and legal boundary

HAI’s requested parent-permission condition for ages 13–17 is a product rule; COPPA’s core parental-consent requirements concern covered collection of personal information from children under 13. Do not describe COPPA as requiring parental consent for every user under 18. Whether COPPA applies depends on the service’s audience and data practices: the FTC considers multiple factors and actual/intended audience. HAI’s owner should document the audience classification for each product using those factors; qualified legal review is recommended as needed based on the service facts and applicable jurisdictions. A general-audience service is not automatically “mixed audience” merely because children use it. A mixed-audience service is a subset of child-directed services and cannot exclude under-13 children altogether; primarily child-directed services generally must treat all users as children. The owner’s operating scope (“United States and its territories”) is not a determination of entity domicile, governing law, or every state/territory rule.

The current Rule covers commercial operators in U.S. territories as well as states and the District of Columbia. The amended Rule’s general compliance date was April 22, 2026 and has passed. Georgia residence does not by itself establish HAI’s legal entity, governing law, or the complete law applicable to users across the stated service scope. This is a source-based process proposal, not a finding that either product is or is not COPPA-covered, nor a certification of compliance. See the [current COPPA Rule, 16 CFR Part 312](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312), the [FTC’s COPPA FAQs](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions), and the [2025 final-rule publication and dates](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule).

## Selected flow and implementation notes (not public policy)

1. **Before new account information:** The selected local flow asks for date of birth before registration details, OAuth account creation, or profile creation. DOB is not part of a profile/session response or public page. Both browser entry points still start performance RUM before the age screen; do not claim zero pre-age collection. [FTC guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions) recommends neutral age screening in relevant cases; the eCFR has a specific pre-age collection restriction for mixed-audience services and limited exceptions.
2. **18 or older:** Continue through ordinary registration/OAuth, subject to the approved terms and applicable law.
3. **Ages 13–17:** The local implementation keeps the applicant inactive and creates no enabled account, profile, or session until a parent/guardian attests and the applicant completes the separate continuation link. Guardian GET is non-consuming; affirmative POST consumes the one-use link. A separate private guardian control supports withdrawal and retries applicant email without revealing the applicant token. The attestation records a claim, not proof, and does not independently verify identity or guardianship. No ID collection or manual review is selected. Email verification, verified OAuth email, and `AUTH_AUTOVERIFY` do not satisfy guardian attestation. This is not represented as COPPA VPC. No parent access to private messages, account content, or a guardian dashboard is implemented or implied. The notice/scope and whether changed data practices require renewed permission still need owner review.

   **Selected direction and current implementation:** store DOB privately in the permission row, account reference after attachment, guardian contact, attestation text/version, timestamps, and status. A derived private eighteenth-birthday date is kept on the account only while age restriction applies and is cleared at 18. Retain the permission record only while permission is needed; delete within 90 days after withdrawal, closure, or the user turning 18. Unapproved requests expire within 90 days after creation. Avoid identity documents and video. This product rule and implementation do not establish legal sufficiency; verification and owner review remain open.
4. **Under 13:** Block account creation as the selected product rule. Do not collect account/profile information and do not route the applicant to a guardian-consent flow. Separately document intended-audience classification and resolve any specific legal question it raises; that assessment does not silently change the selected product rule. If a service is child-directed or mixed-audience, Terms language alone does not change that classification, and the participation requirements may differ; any different participation approach requires a new owner decision and separately designed process. Do not collect ordinary account details and then send the user to ask a parent.
5. **Declined, pending-expired, or revoked:** Keep the applicant/user out of enabled account/session state; withdrawal denies minor access and revokes active sessions. The selected 90-day product retention directions are to delete unapproved pending requests 90 days after creation and delete the minimal permission record within 90 days after withdrawal, closure, or the user turning 18. These are not statutory periods. The owner selected the minimal active record only while needed to enforce the exception. Raw identity documents, video, or other verification artifacts are outside the selected 90-day schedule and must not be collected or retained by default. Confirm each implemented record field, event timestamp, and deletion trigger, then prove deletion is enforceable. COPPA’s under-13 notice/consent exception requires deletion after a reasonable time if consent is not obtained; that exception does not set a fixed number of days. Do not promise account erasure: full account deletion/export remains deferred as DF-007, and the profile delete route returns 501 for an owned app-scoped profile because lifecycle handling is unavailable ([RR11-I01](./RR-11-I01.md)). Until a tested lifecycle path exists, do not admit users whose access depends on permission HAI cannot honor or revoke safely.

For covered under-13 data, COPPA requires prior verifiable parental consent before collection/use/disclosure, subject to narrow exceptions. The Rule requires direct parent notice and an online children’s privacy notice; it provides parent rights to review information, refuse further use/future collection, and direct deletion. It also requires a written retention policy with purpose, business need, and deletion timeframe; no indefinite retention. Email-based consent methods in the Rule carry conditions, including additional assurance steps and, where permitted, no disclosure of children’s personal information. Select no method here: legal/technical review must match the method to actual disclosures and prove prompt deletion/security where the method requires it. See [16 CFR §§312.4–312.10](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312).
