# RR-11 — Minor eligibility and parental-permission process (internal draft)

**Status:** Proposal for owner/legal/operations review; not approved policy, legal advice, a working process, or public copy. No account-flow changes are implemented.

**Date:** 2026-10-14

**Scope:** Proposed age bands, consent states, and acceptance gates for Optimistic Tanuki and Towne Square only. Do not apply this decision to other HAI products without an explicit owner decision.

> The owner selected eligibility for adults 18+ and users ages 13–17 only with explicit parent/guardian permission; users under 13 are unsupported under HAI’s selected policy. HAI is based in Georgia, and the user/operator owns monitoring of `support@hopefulaspirationsindustries.com`. The owner confirms the mailbox is set up and receives email. The owner selected a simple parent/guardian attestation as the draft method for the 13–17 product rule, without independent identity or guardianship verification. This records a person’s claim of authority and permission, not proof of either. The owner selected a 90-day retention direction for unapproved pending requests and minimal permission records after withdrawal/closure, as described below. Intake routing, consent handling, and the minor-permission process remain unreviewed and unimplemented; no personal operator name is inferred. This draft does not approve admission of minors or publicize the address.

## Decision and legal boundary

HAI’s requested parent-permission condition for ages 13–17 is a product rule; COPPA’s core parental-consent requirements concern covered collection of personal information from children under 13. Do not describe COPPA as requiring parental consent for every user under 18. Whether COPPA applies depends on the service’s audience and data practices: the FTC considers multiple factors and actual/intended audience. HAI’s owner should document the audience classification for each product using those factors; qualified legal review is recommended as needed based on the service facts and applicable jurisdictions. A general-audience service is not automatically “mixed audience” merely because children use it. A mixed-audience service is a subset of child-directed services and cannot exclude under-13 children altogether; primarily child-directed services generally must treat all users as children. The owner’s operating scope (“United States and its territories”) is not a determination of entity domicile, governing law, or every state/territory rule.

The current Rule covers commercial operators in U.S. territories as well as states and the District of Columbia. The amended Rule’s general compliance date was April 22, 2026 and has passed. Georgia residence does not by itself establish HAI’s legal entity, governing law, or the complete law applicable to users across the stated service scope. This is a source-based process proposal, not a finding that either product is or is not COPPA-covered, nor a certification of compliance. See the [current COPPA Rule, 16 CFR Part 312](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312), the [FTC’s COPPA FAQs](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions), and the [2025 final-rule publication and dates](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule).

## Recommended process for owner review (not implemented)

1. **Before account information:** Present neutral age entry before account/profile fields, registration, or OAuth account creation. Do not preselect an eligible age or invite a user to misstate age. Prefer calculating the selected band and discarding raw birth-date input unless an approved data map identifies a specific need to retain it. Review pre-age telemetry and third-party requests: current source shows both browser entry points start performance RUM, so the age screen alone does not establish that no personal information is collected before age. [FTC guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions) recommends neutral age screening in relevant cases; the eCFR has a specific pre-age collection restriction for mixed-audience services and limited exceptions.
2. **18 or older:** Continue through ordinary registration/OAuth, subject to the approved terms and applicable law.
3. **Ages 13–17:** Keep registration in a pending state; do not create an enabled account, establish a session, or permit ordinary collection/use until the guardian attestation is recorded through a reviewed and tested process. The owner selected a simple parent/guardian attestation as the draft method for HAI’s product rule; it does not independently verify identity or guardianship and records a claim of authority and permission, not proof. Do not add email-confirmation, manual-review, identity-document, video, or third-party verification requirements unless separately selected. This attestation is not represented as COPPA verifiable parental consent (VPC). Keep permission scope narrow: no parent access to private messages or routine account content; no ongoing monitoring or guardian dashboard is implied. Store only the minimal permission record needed to enforce the approved rule. The owner should decide when a change to data practices requires renewed permission and make that scope clear in the permission notice.
4. **Under 13:** If HAI’s owner-documented audience classification supports the selected exclusion, the proposed process denies account creation and collects no account/profile information. Otherwise, hold the exclusion and resolve the service’s participation approach before enabling registration. If a service is child-directed, Terms language alone does not change that classification; if it is mixed-audience or child-directed, COPPA does not allow an age gate to exclude under-13 children from participating. The operator would need a separately designed participation path with required notice and VPC before covered collection/use/disclosure, or must not operate that service in a form that excludes children. Do not collect ordinary account details and then send the user to ask a parent.
5. **Declined, unverified, expired, or revoked:** Keep the user out of enabled account/session state. The owner’s 90-day retention direction is to delete unapproved pending requests 90 days after creation, and to delete the minimal permission record 90 days after withdrawal or closure. This is an owner-selected product retention direction, not a statutory period; active permission-record retention remains unresolved. Raw identity documents, video, or other verification artifacts are not included in this 90-day schedule and must not be collected or retained by default. Before implementation, define each record, event timestamp, and deletion trigger, then prove deletion is enforceable. COPPA’s under-13 notice/consent exception requires deletion after a reasonable time if consent is not obtained; that exception does not set a fixed number of days. Do not promise account erasure: full account deletion/export remains deferred as DF-007, and the profile delete route returns 501 for an owned app-scoped profile because lifecycle handling is unavailable ([RR11-I01](./RR-11-I01.md)). Until a tested lifecycle path exists, do not admit users whose access depends on permission HAI cannot honor or revoke safely.

For covered under-13 data, COPPA requires prior verifiable parental consent before collection/use/disclosure, subject to narrow exceptions. The Rule requires direct parent notice and an online children’s privacy notice; it provides parent rights to review information, refuse further use/future collection, and direct deletion. It also requires a written retention policy with purpose, business need, and deletion timeframe; no indefinite retention. Email-based consent methods in the Rule carry conditions, including additional assurance steps and, where permitted, no disclosure of children’s personal information. Select no method here: legal/technical review must match the method to actual disclosures and prove prompt deletion/security where the method requires it. See [16 CFR §§312.4–312.10](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312).

## Open decisions and acceptance gates

Before any minor flow or public eligibility statement is approved, review and decide:

- The owner’s written audience classification for each product using the FTC’s subject matter, visuals, child-oriented features/incentives, language, actual audience, intended audience, and other relevant factors. Qualified legal review is recommended where the classification or the proposed under-13 exclusion raises jurisdiction-specific questions; it is not treated here as a statutory approval step.
- The owner confirms `support@hopefulaspirationsindustries.com` is set up, receives email, and is monitored by the user/operator. Before relying on it for guardian permission, establish intake routing, access controls, backup coverage, and separation of consent/privacy requests from ordinary support and abuse/copyright reports. No personal operator name or service commitment is supplied. The mailbox alone does not establish guardian verification, consent records, revocation, or deletion handling.
- Translate the selected simple parent/guardian attestation into a reviewed notice and record schema. It records a claim of authority and permission, not independent proof of identity or guardianship. Do not add email confirmation, manual review, or another verification layer unless separately selected. Decide permission scope, withdrawal, stale-request handling, and event logging. Do not describe the attestation as COPPA VPC without separate legal/technical qualification; stronger verification options can be reconsidered if product or legal review requires them.
- Implement the owner-selected retention direction: delete unapproved pending requests 90 days after creation and delete the minimal permission record 90 days after withdrawal or closure. This is not a statutory retention period. Active permission-record retention and exact field/event definitions remain unresolved. Raw identity documents/video are not included and must not be collected or retained by default. Prove the deletion path before relying on the schedule.
- A demonstrated pre-consent boundary for email/password registration and OAuth, server-side denial of unapproved minor access, parent notice/rights, session disablement after revocation, child-data retention/deletion behavior, and applicable service-provider/third-party controls. Current OT and TS registration components submit account data directly; OAuth registration can establish a session. The reviewed flows contain no age, guardian-permission, or consent-record gate ([OT registration](../../../apps/client-interface/src/app/components/register.component.ts), [TS registration](../../../apps/local-hub/src/app/pages/register/register.component.ts)).
- A child/minor-data inventory, approved retention/deletion schedule, security owner and controls, and verified intake routing before publication. The owner confirms email receipt; no separate routing or consent-process test is recorded. The existing RUM note and broader account lifecycle gaps in [RR11-I02/I03 plan](./RR-11-I02-I03-plan.md) remain applicable.

These gates require a separately scoped implementation plan spanning shared registration UI, password and OAuth entry points, server-side pending/approval enforcement, consent persistence, session revocation, and the parent-request/deletion lifecycle. The future slice should reuse shared auth infrastructure where feasible, add explicit tests for bypass and expiry/revocation behavior, and include an approved child-data map and deletion/readback proof. This work is not an addition to RR11-I03’s public-page-only scope and does not close DF-007’s full account deletion/export work; map dependencies and propose any new RR identifier before implementation. Keep public copy held until the process is staffed, tested, and approved. The [FTC’s February 2026 age-verification enforcement statement](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/enforcement-policy-statement-promoting-adoption-age-verification-technology) is a limited enforcement policy with strict purpose, deletion, notice, security, accuracy, and third-party conditions; it is not blanket permission to collect age-verification data or a substitute for consent.

**Disposition:** Owner’s age-band direction recorded; legal classification, implementation, contact operation, and publication are unresolved. No minor eligibility or COPPA compliance claim is approved.
