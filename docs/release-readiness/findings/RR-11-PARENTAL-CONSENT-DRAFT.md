# RR-11 — Minor eligibility and parental-permission process (internal draft)

**Status:** Internal policy/process draft; the limited new-registration DOB gate and anonymous one-use guardian-link flow are implemented in the current working tree with adult/under-13/teen browser flows verified locally. The newly approved guardian-owned linked view/manage flow is not implemented; the current-source active-socket withdrawal check passed on the isolated stack. This is not approved public policy, legal advice, a deployed release, or RR-11 acceptance.

**Date:** 2026-10-14

**Scope:** Owner-selected draft age direction and proposed consent states/acceptance gates for Optimistic Tanuki and Towne Square only. Do not apply this direction to other HAI products without an explicit owner decision.

> The owner selected both apps as mainly adult-oriented while allowing eligible teens: adults 18+; users ages 13–17 only with parent/guardian permission; under 13 unsupported. Private, self-reported DOB is used only for new registrations; existing accounts remain on the legacy path. One simple parent/guardian attestation applies across both apps without independent identity or guardianship verification. A guardian must create their own account, link it to the teen’s pending request before activation, and attest to grant access; no teen account exists before permission. Policy changes trigger guardian notification, not renewed attestation. No tracking may run before the age check. The current implementation provides a private one-use guardian link but does not implement the required guardian account/link/view-manage flow. The permission record retains DOB, guardian contact, attestation text/version, timestamps, status, and an account reference after attachment while permission is needed; it is deleted within 90 days after withdrawal, closure, or age 18. Unapproved pending requests expire within 90 days of creation. This is not blanket publication approval. The support inbox is not the consent route.

## Decision and legal boundary

HAI’s requested parent-permission condition for ages 13–17 is a product rule; COPPA’s core parental-consent requirements concern covered collection of personal information from children under 13. Do not describe COPPA as requiring parental consent for every user under 18. Whether COPPA applies depends on the service’s audience and data practices: the FTC considers multiple factors and actual/intended audience. HAI’s owner should document the audience classification for each product using those factors; qualified legal review is recommended as needed based on the service facts and applicable jurisdictions. A general-audience service is not automatically “mixed audience” merely because children use it. A mixed-audience service is a subset of child-directed services and cannot exclude under-13 children altogether; primarily child-directed services generally must treat all users as children. The owner’s operating scope (“United States and its territories”) is not a determination of entity domicile, governing law, or every state/territory rule.

The current Rule covers commercial operators in U.S. territories as well as states and the District of Columbia. The amended Rule’s general compliance date was April 22, 2026 and has passed. Georgia residence does not by itself establish HAI’s legal entity, governing law, or the complete law applicable to users across the stated service scope. This is a source-based process proposal, not a finding that either product is or is not COPPA-covered, nor a certification of compliance. See the [current COPPA Rule, 16 CFR Part 312](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312), the [FTC’s COPPA FAQs](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions), and the [2025 final-rule publication and dates](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule).

## Selected flow and implementation notes (not public policy)

1. **Before new account information:** The selected local flow asks for date of birth before registration details, OAuth account creation, or profile creation. DOB is not part of a profile/session response or public page. Owner direction is that no tracking runs before the age check, and performance RUM stays disabled until the age/consent screen is completed. Current source starts RUM before that screen, so this is an open implementation gap, not current behavior. [FTC guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions) recommends neutral age screening in relevant cases; the eCFR has a specific pre-age collection restriction for mixed-audience services and limited exceptions.
2. **18 or older:** Continue through ordinary registration/OAuth, subject to the approved terms and applicable law.
3. **Ages 13–17:** The current local flow keeps a pending request inactive and creates no teen account/profile/session until guardian attestation and applicant continuation. The required guardian-linked view/manage account flow is not implemented. Guardian GET is non-consuming; affirmative POST consumes the one-use link. A separate private guardian control supports withdrawal and retries applicant email without revealing the applicant token. The attestation records a claim, not proof, and does not independently verify identity or guardianship. No ID collection, email confirmation, or manual review is selected. Email verification, verified OAuth email, and `AUTH_AUTOVERIFY` do not satisfy guardian attestation. This is not represented as COPPA VPC. The guardian must have their own account and link it to the pending dependent request before account activation. The attestation grants access after the teen completes registration. Linked guardian access includes viewing and managing the dependent account’s posts, settings, and content (including create, edit, and delete actions). These actions are attributed to the guardian. Do not use shared passwords or full account impersonation. The guardian link/access ends when the dependent turns 18. Policy changes require notification, not renewed attestation.

   **Selected direction and current implementation:** store DOB privately in the permission row, account reference after attachment, guardian contact, attestation text/version, timestamps, and status. A derived private eighteenth-birthday date is kept on the account only while age restriction applies and is cleared at 18. Retain the permission record only while permission is needed; delete within 90 days after withdrawal, closure, or the user turning 18. Unapproved requests expire within 90 days after creation. Avoid identity documents and video. This product rule and implementation do not establish legal sufficiency; verification and owner review remain open.
4. **Under 13:** Block account creation as the selected product rule. Do not collect account/profile information and do not route the applicant to a guardian-consent flow. The owner-selected audience direction is mainly adult-oriented with eligible teens allowed; separately resolve any specific legal question this product direction raises; that assessment does not silently change the selected product rule. If a service is child-directed or mixed-audience, Terms language alone does not change that classification, and the participation requirements may differ; any different participation approach requires a new owner decision and separately designed process. Do not collect ordinary account details and then send the user to ask a parent.
5. **Declined, pending-expired, or revoked:** Keep the applicant/user out of enabled account/session state; withdrawal denies minor access and revokes active sessions. The selected 90-day product retention directions are to delete unapproved pending requests 90 days after creation and delete the minimal permission record within 90 days after withdrawal, closure, or the user turning 18. These are not statutory periods. The owner selected the minimal active record only while needed to enforce the exception. Raw identity documents, video, or other verification artifacts are outside the selected 90-day schedule and must not be collected or retained by default. Confirm each implemented record field, event timestamp, and deletion trigger, then prove deletion is enforceable. COPPA’s under-13 notice/consent exception requires deletion after a reasonable time if consent is not obtained; that exception does not set a fixed number of days. Do not promise account erasure before implementation and acceptance: full account deletion/export is required by owner direction and remains unimplemented under mandatory DF-007, and the profile delete route returns 501 for an owned app-scoped profile because lifecycle handling is unavailable ([RR11-I01](./RR-11-I01.md)). Until a tested lifecycle path exists, do not admit users whose access depends on permission HAI cannot honor or revoke safely.

For covered under-13 data, COPPA requires prior verifiable parental consent before collection/use/disclosure, subject to narrow exceptions. The Rule requires direct parent notice and an online children’s privacy notice; it provides parent rights to review information, refuse further use/future collection, and direct deletion. It also requires a written retention policy with purpose, business need, and deletion timeframe; no indefinite retention. Email-based consent methods in the Rule carry conditions, including additional assurance steps and, where permitted, no disclosure of children’s personal information. Select no method here: legal/technical review must match the method to actual disclosures and prove prompt deletion/security where the method requires it. See [16 CFR §§312.4–312.10](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312).

> **Give permission for your teen to use Optimistic Tanuki and Towne Square**
>
> To give permission, create your own guardian account and confirm that you are the teen’s parent or guardian. We will link your account to the teen’s pending request; the teen’s account stays inactive until you give permission and they complete registration. Your attestation gives you access to view and manage the teen’s posts, settings, and content, including creating, editing, and deleting content. Actions you take are attributed to you as the guardian. Do not share passwords; you do not sign in as the teen. You may withdraw permission from your guardian account; withdrawal disables the teen’s account in both apps and ends your access. Your access is also removed automatically when the teen turns 18. We will notify you when our permission policy changes; you do not need to attest again for a policy change. We do not require identity documents or independently verify your identity or guardianship. We keep only a minimal permission record, including your contact information and the teen’s date of birth, while permission is needed. We delete it within 90 days after permission is withdrawn, the account is closed, or the teen turns 18. Unapproved requests expire within 90 days.

The exact wording is owner-approved, but it does not describe current functionality or prove publication/deployment. The current private one-use-link flow does not support a guardian-owned linked account. Implement the approved flow and verify it before publishing or claiming availability.
