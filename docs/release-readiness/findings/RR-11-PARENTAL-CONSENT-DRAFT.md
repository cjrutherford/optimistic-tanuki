# RR-11 — Minor eligibility and parental-permission process (internal draft)

**Status:** Proposal for owner/legal/operations review; not approved policy, legal advice, a working process, or public copy. No account-flow changes are implemented.

**Date:** 2026-10-14

**Scope:** Owner-selected draft age direction and proposed consent states/acceptance gates for Optimistic Tanuki and Towne Square only. Do not apply this direction to other HAI products without an explicit owner decision.

> The owner selected the same age direction for OT and TS: adults 18+; users ages 13–17 only with explicit parent/guardian permission; under 13 unsupported. The selected draft method is simple parent/guardian attestation without independent identity or guardianship verification. It records a claim of authority and permission, not proof. The owner selected a minimal active permission record with account reference, guardian contact, attestation text/version, timestamps, and status, retained only while permission is needed and deleted within 90 days after withdrawal, closure, or age 18. Unapproved pending requests are deleted 90 days after creation. These are selected draft directions, not an implemented process or blanket publication approval. Intake routing, consent handling, and the minor-permission process remain unreviewed and unimplemented. This draft does not approve admission of minors or publicize the address.

## Decision and legal boundary

HAI’s requested parent-permission condition for ages 13–17 is a product rule; COPPA’s core parental-consent requirements concern covered collection of personal information from children under 13. Do not describe COPPA as requiring parental consent for every user under 18. Whether COPPA applies depends on the service’s audience and data practices: the FTC considers multiple factors and actual/intended audience. HAI’s owner should document the audience classification for each product using those factors; qualified legal review is recommended as needed based on the service facts and applicable jurisdictions. A general-audience service is not automatically “mixed audience” merely because children use it. A mixed-audience service is a subset of child-directed services and cannot exclude under-13 children altogether; primarily child-directed services generally must treat all users as children. The owner’s operating scope (“United States and its territories”) is not a determination of entity domicile, governing law, or every state/territory rule.

The current Rule covers commercial operators in U.S. territories as well as states and the District of Columbia. The amended Rule’s general compliance date was April 22, 2026 and has passed. Georgia residence does not by itself establish HAI’s legal entity, governing law, or the complete law applicable to users across the stated service scope. This is a source-based process proposal, not a finding that either product is or is not COPPA-covered, nor a certification of compliance. See the [current COPPA Rule, 16 CFR Part 312](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312), the [FTC’s COPPA FAQs](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions), and the [2025 final-rule publication and dates](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule).

## Recommended process for owner review (not implemented)

1. **Before account information:** Present neutral age entry before account/profile fields, registration, or OAuth account creation. Do not preselect an eligible age or invite a user to misstate age. The age-input mechanism remains pending: the owner must choose private DOB entry with derived age handling or another age-band/transition method before schema design. Review pre-age telemetry and third-party requests: current source shows both browser entry points start performance RUM, so the age screen alone does not establish that no personal information is collected before age. [FTC guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions) recommends neutral age screening in relevant cases; the eCFR has a specific pre-age collection restriction for mixed-audience services and limited exceptions.
2. **18 or older:** Continue through ordinary registration/OAuth, subject to the approved terms and applicable law.
3. **Ages 13–17:** Keep the applicant inactive and registration pending; do not create an enabled account, profile, or session, or permit ordinary collection/use until a parent/guardian attestation is recorded. The selected mechanism is a private one-use guardian link followed by simple attestation. It does not independently verify identity or guardianship and records a claim of authority and permission, not proof. No ID collection or manual review is selected. Do not treat email verification, verified OAuth email, or `AUTH_AUTOVERIFY` as guardian attestation. This attestation is not represented as COPPA verifiable parental consent (VPC). Keep permission scope narrow: no parent access to private messages or routine account content; no ongoing monitoring or guardian dashboard is implied. The selected minimal-record fields and lifecycle below remain draft directions requiring review before implementation. Permission scope and when changed data practices require renewed permission must be made clear in the notice.

   **Selected draft direction:** store account reference, guardian contact, attestation text/version, timestamps, and status. Retain the minimal record only while permission is needed; delete within 90 days after withdrawal, closure, or the user turning 18. Avoid identity documents and video. These fields and lifecycle require review and tested deletion before implementation; this direction does not establish legal sufficiency.
4. **Under 13:** Block account creation as the selected product rule. Do not collect account/profile information and do not route the applicant to a guardian-consent flow. Separately document intended-audience classification and resolve any specific legal question it raises; that assessment does not silently change the selected product rule. If a service is child-directed or mixed-audience, Terms language alone does not change that classification, and the participation requirements may differ; any different participation approach requires a new owner decision and separately designed process. Do not collect ordinary account details and then send the user to ask a parent.
5. **Declined, pending-expired, or revoked:** Keep the applicant/user out of enabled account/session state; withdrawal denies minor access and revokes active sessions. The selected 90-day product retention directions are to delete unapproved pending requests 90 days after creation and delete the minimal permission record within 90 days after withdrawal, closure, or the user turning 18. These are not statutory periods. The owner selected the minimal active record only while needed to enforce the exception. Raw identity documents, video, or other verification artifacts are outside the selected 90-day schedule and must not be collected or retained by default. Before implementation, define each record, event timestamp, and deletion trigger, then prove deletion is enforceable. COPPA’s under-13 notice/consent exception requires deletion after a reasonable time if consent is not obtained; that exception does not set a fixed number of days. Do not promise account erasure: full account deletion/export remains deferred as DF-007, and the profile delete route returns 501 for an owned app-scoped profile because lifecycle handling is unavailable ([RR11-I01](./RR-11-I01.md)). Until a tested lifecycle path exists, do not admit users whose access depends on permission HAI cannot honor or revoke safely.

For covered under-13 data, COPPA requires prior verifiable parental consent before collection/use/disclosure, subject to narrow exceptions. The Rule requires direct parent notice and an online children’s privacy notice; it provides parent rights to review information, refuse further use/future collection, and direct deletion. It also requires a written retention policy with purpose, business need, and deletion timeframe; no indefinite retention. Email-based consent methods in the Rule carry conditions, including additional assurance steps and, where permitted, no disclosure of children’s personal information. Select no method here: legal/technical review must match the method to actual disclosures and prove prompt deletion/security where the method requires it. See [16 CFR §§312.4–312.10](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312).

## Open decisions and acceptance gates

Before any minor flow or public eligibility statement is approved, review and decide:

- The owner selected the age direction for both OT and TS. Three implementation choices remain unanswered: (1) DOB-based age determination and age-18 transition versus another private age-band/transition method; (2) how to gate existing accounts with unknown age at cutover versus new registrations only; and (3) whether an attestation is shared across both apps or scoped to the app in which it was granted. Do not settle these in schema or code. Document the intended-audience classification for each app using relevant product facts; qualified legal advice may be appropriate if classification or applicable law is uncertain, but this draft does not require lawyer sign-off.
- The owner confirms `support@hopefulaspirationsindustries.com` is set up, receives email, and is monitored by the user/operator. Before relying on it for guardian permission, establish intake routing, access controls, backup coverage, and separation of consent/privacy requests from ordinary support and abuse/copyright reports. No personal operator name or service commitment is supplied. The mailbox alone does not establish guardian verification, consent records, revocation, or deletion handling.
- Translate the selected private one-use link and simple parent/guardian attestation into a reviewed notice and minimal record schema. It records a claim of authority and permission, not independent proof of identity or guardianship. No ID collection, email-confirmation, or manual-review step was selected. Decide permission scope, withdrawal, stale-request handling, and event logging. Do not describe the attestation as COPPA VPC without separate legal/technical qualification.
- Preserve the selected product retention directions: delete unapproved pending requests 90 days after creation; retain the selected minimal active record only while permission is needed and delete it within 90 days after withdrawal, closure, or the user turning 18. These are not statutory periods. Review exact field/event definitions and verify deletion behavior before relying on the schedule. Do not collect raw identity/video artifacts by default.
- A demonstrated pre-consent boundary for email/password registration and OAuth, server-side denial of unapproved minor access, parent notice/rights, session disablement after revocation, child-data retention/deletion behavior, and applicable service-provider/third-party controls. Current OT and TS registration components submit account data directly; OAuth registration can establish a session. The reviewed flows contain no age, guardian-permission, or consent-record gate ([OT registration](../../../apps/client-interface/src/app/components/register.component.ts), [TS registration](../../../apps/local-hub/src/app/pages/register/register.component.ts)).
- A child/minor-data inventory, approved retention/deletion schedule, security owner and controls, and verified intake routing before publication. The owner confirms email receipt; no separate routing or consent-process test is recorded. The existing RUM note and broader account lifecycle gaps in [RR11-I02/I03 plan](./RR-11-I02-I03-plan.md) remain applicable.

The linked [implementation plan](./RR-11-I02-I03-plan.md#selected-age-direction--bounded-follow-on-implementation-before-rr11-v01) maps the exact shared UI, DTO, authentication-service, gateway, test, and generated-migration surfaces. The source slice remains separate from RR11-I03’s public-page-only scope and does not close DF-007’s full account deletion/export work. Keep public copy held until the process is staffed, tested, and approved. The [FTC’s February 2026 age-verification enforcement statement](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/enforcement-policy-statement-promoting-adoption-age-verification-technology) is a limited enforcement policy with strict purpose, deletion, notice, security, accuracy, and third-party conditions; it is not blanket permission to collect age-verification data or a substitute for consent.
